Stretching routines for runners

A good warm-up gets you running freely from the first kilometre, and a short stretch afterwards helps you recover for the next one. Here are the two runners' routines from the Movage app, with photos and videos of every move.

Before a run about 9 min

Dynamic moves raise your heart rate and take your hips and ankles through the range you'll use when you run. Keep them moving rather than holding still.

Leg swings

Hold something sturdy for balance, like a wall, door frame or chair back. Swing one straight leg forward and back, gradually higher. Then do side to side.

Time: 40s each side

Standing hip circles

Stand tall holding something for balance if you need it. Lift one knee to hip height, open it out to the side, then lower it behind you in a big slow circle. Then reverse.

Time: 40s each side

Walking lunge with a twist

Step into a long lunge, back knee just above the floor. Rotate your chest over the front leg with arms out, then stand and step through into the next lunge. Tall and controlled.

Time: 45s

World's greatest stretch

Step into a long lunge and put both hands on the floor inside the front foot. Drop the inside elbow towards the instep, then rotate and reach that arm to the ceiling. Hand back down, straighten the front leg, repeat.

Time: 60s each side

Knee-to-wall rocks

Half-kneel facing a wall. Drive the front knee towards the wall over your little toes, heel down, then back. Rest a weight on the knee to load it if you have one.

Time: 45s each side

Glute bridge

Lie on your back, knees bent, feet flat. Squeeze your glutes and lift your hips until knees, hips and shoulders line up. Ribs down, no arching.

Time: 45s

A-skips

Skip forwards, driving one knee up to hip height while the other foot pushes off the ball of the foot. Opposite arm swings forward. Light, quick and bouncy.

Time: 30s

Ankle pogo hops

Small, quick hops on the balls of your feet with knees almost straight, like a bouncing spring. Stay light and quiet. Wakes up your calves and Achilles before a run.

Time: 30s

After a run about 14 min

Once you've cooled down, hold these stretches to ease off the calves, quads, hips and hamstrings that did the work.

Wall calf stretch

Hands on a wall, one leg back with the knee straight and heel down. Lean in until you feel the calf.

Time: 45s each side

Bent-knee calf stretch

Same as the wall calf stretch, but bend the back knee and sink down, keeping the heel on the floor. You'll feel it lower in the calf.

Time: 45s each side

Standing quad stretch

Stand tall holding a wall, fence or chair back. Bend one knee and hold that ankle behind you, pulling the heel towards your bum. Knees together, hips pushed slightly forward.

Time: 45s each side

Half-kneeling hip flexor stretch

Half-kneel, back knee under hip. Tuck your pelvis, squeeze the back glute and shift forward slightly. Reach the same-side arm overhead for more.

Time: 45s each side

Lying hamstring stretch

On your back, loop a towel or belt around one foot. Lift the straight leg until you feel the back of the thigh, and hold. Other leg flat, lower back relaxed.

Time: 60s each side

Lying figure four

Lie on your back, cross one ankle over the opposite knee and pull that thigh towards your chest. Keep your head and shoulders relaxed on the floor.

Time: 60s each side

Standing side bend (IT band)

Cross one foot behind the other. Reach the arm on the same side as the back leg up and over your head, and lean away to feel the outside of that hip. Hips stay square.

Time: 45s each side

Pigeon

Front shin across your body, back leg long behind. Square your hips and fold forward over the front leg. Support with a cushion under the hip if needed.

Time: 60s each side

Child's pose reach

Kneel, sit back on your heels, walk your hands forward and let your chest sink to the floor. Slow breaths into your back.

Time: 60s

Where runners are usually tight

Running asks a lot of the ankles, calves, hip flexors and hamstrings. The knee to wall test checks your ankles, the Thomas test your hip flexors and the straight leg raise your hamstrings. Find your tightest area and add the matching stretches from our hip mobility exercises.
