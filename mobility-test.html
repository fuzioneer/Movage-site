Mobility test: 12 tests explained

A mobility test measures how far your joints can move, with control, from shoulders and upper back to hips and ankles. Here are the 12 tests Movage uses, what each one tells you, how to do it at home and what a good result looks like.

The six mobility zones

Movage groups the tests into six zones and gives each one a score. Your overall mobility score out of 100 combines them, so you can see at a glance where you move well and where you are tight.

  • Shoulders
  • Overhead
  • Thorax
  • Posterior chain
  • Hips
  • Ankles

Quick test or full test?

The quick test uses one test per zone (the six marked "Quick test" below) and takes about 8 minutes, short enough to repeat every couple of weeks. The full test runs all 12, measures left and right separately and takes about 17 minutes. Do it first, then every few months.

Shoulders tests

1. Back scratch Quick test

What it measures: Shoulder rotation both ways at once: one hand reaches over the shoulder and the other comes up behind the back.

Often called the Apley scratch test. A big gap shows tight internal or external rotation, and a difference between sides is common in racket and throwing sports.

How to do it: Stand with your back to the camera, whole upper body in view. Reach one hand over the shoulder and the other up behind your back, fingers pointing at each other. Hold 2 seconds, then swap hands. A gap reads minus, an overlap plus. 'Right' means right hand on top.

Good result: Full marks when the fingers overlap by 5 cm or more. Measured on each side.

2. Shoulder external rotation

What it measures: How far the upper arm can rotate backwards with the elbow at 90 degrees.

External rotation keeps the shoulder healthy overhead and in the front rack position. Desk posture and lots of pressing tend to reduce it.

How to do it: Lie on your back, side-on to the camera with the phone at floor level by the arm you're testing. Upper arm out to the side at shoulder height, elbow bent 90°, forearm pointing at the ceiling. Let the hand fall back towards the floor without the shoulder lifting. Hold 2 seconds. Then turn round for the other arm.

Good result: Full marks at 90°. Measured on each side.

Overhead tests

3. Shoulder flexion Quick test

What it measures: How far you can raise a straight arm overhead.

Tight lats, chest or a stiff upper back stop the arm short, so you arch your lower back to get overhead. It matters for pressing, pull-ups, swimming and simply reaching a high shelf.

How to do it: Stand side-on to the camera. Ribs down, lower back neutral. Raise a straight arm overhead as far as it goes, hold 2 seconds, lower. Turn round for the other side. 180° is fully overhead.

Good result: Full marks at 180°. Measured on each side.

Thorax tests

4. Seated thoracic rotation Quick test

What it measures: How far your upper back (thoracic spine) twists while your hips stay still.

A mobile mid back takes load off the lower back and neck. Golfers, runners and anyone who sits a lot benefit from better rotation.

How to do it: Sit tall on a chair without arms, facing the camera, feet flat and knees together. Rest a broomstick or rolled towel across your shoulders. Hold still facing forward for 2 seconds so the camera can learn your shoulder width, then rotate as far as you can each way, keeping your hips and knees facing forward. Hold each end 2 seconds.

Good result: Full marks at 50°. Measured on each side.

Posterior chain tests

5. Straight leg raise

What it measures: How high one straight leg lifts while lying on your back.

This isolates the hamstrings and the back of the leg without the lower back joining in, unlike a toe touch. Under 70 degrees usually means tight hamstrings.

How to do it: Lie on your back side-on to the camera, legs straight. Raise one leg as high as you can, knee locked, other leg flat. Repeat with the other leg. 90° is vertical.

Good result: Full marks at 90°. Measured on each side.

6. Standing toe touch Quick test

What it measures: How close your fingertips get to the floor with straight knees.

The classic sit and reach style test of the whole posterior chain: calves, hamstrings and lower back working together.

How to do it: Stand side-on to the camera on a flat floor, feet together and knees straight. Reach down towards the floor as far as you can and hold 2 seconds. Fingertips touching the floor is 0. If you stop short, the gap to the floor is a minus number, for example -10 cm.

Good result: Full marks when your fingertips touch the floor.

Hips tests

7. Hip internal rotation

What it measures: How far your hip turns inwards, measured by how far the foot swings out while seated.

Limited internal rotation is linked to a stiff squat, hip pinching and lower back strain. It's one of the first things to go when you sit a lot.

How to do it: Sit on something high enough that your feet clear the floor (a table, worktop or high bench), facing the camera, knees bent 90° and hip-width apart. Keep your knees still. A rolled towel or foam roller lightly squeezed between them helps. Test one leg at a time: swing one foot outwards as far as it'll go and hold 2 seconds, then do the other leg. The camera measures the shin's angle from vertical.

Good result: Full marks at 40°. Measured on each side.

8. Thomas test (hip flexor)

What it measures: Whether your hip flexors let the thigh drop below the bench while the other knee is hugged in.

Tight hip flexors tilt the pelvis forward, shorten your running stride and can pull on the lower back. Common for desk workers and cyclists.

How to do it: Sit on the end of a bench, bed or sturdy table, side-on to the camera. Lie back hugging one knee to your chest and let the other leg hang for 5 seconds. Swap. The angle is the hanging thigh from horizontal: minus is good, plus is tight.

Good result: Full marks when the thigh drops 10° below level. Measured on each side.

9. 90/90 hip rotation Quick test

What it measures: Hip rotation in the 90/90 sitting position: how close the front knee gets to the floor.

It checks both hip rotations in one position, a good all round measure of hip mobility for squatting, lunging and sport.

How to do it: Sit in a 90/90 position facing the camera, front shin across your body. Lean forward with a straight back and let the front knee drop. Hold 2 seconds, then switch sides. The camera estimates the gap between the front knee and the floor.

Good result: Full marks when the front knee rests on the floor. Measured on each side.

Ankles tests

10. Ankle dorsiflexion (shin lean)

What it measures: How far your shin can lean forward over the foot with the heel down.

This is ankle dorsiflexion, measured with the camera. Without it your heels lift in a squat and your knees and feet take extra strain when running.

How to do it: Side-on to the camera in a half-kneeling lunge, front heel down. Drive the knee forward over the toes as far as you can without lifting the heel. Swap sides. 40° or more is good.

Good result: Full marks at 45°. Measured on each side.

11. Knee-to-wall Quick test

What it measures: How far your toes can be from a wall while the knee still touches it, heel down.

The standard clinical test of ankle dorsiflexion. Around 10 cm or more is good. Less than that limits squats, lunges and running mechanics.

How to do it: Half-kneel side-on to the camera, whole lower body in view. Keeping the front heel down, drive the knee as far forward over the toes as you can. Hold 2 seconds, then swap legs. The camera estimates the knee-to-wall distance (how far the knee travels past the toes).

Good result: Full marks at 14 cm. Measured on each side.

Movement check

12. Deep squat check

What it measures: Your full deep squat pattern: depth, heel contact, knee and torso position.

Not scored, but it shows how ankles, hips and upper back work together, and which zone to work on first.

How to do it: Side-on to the camera, feet shoulder-width. Squat as deep as you can, heels down, chest up. Pause at the bottom, then stand. Records knee angle, shin lean and torso lean.

Good result: Not scored: it's a movement check.
